During a crisis, social media is a firehose of real-time, on-the-ground information —
and almost none of it is structured. This project asked whether you could mine that
firehose to figure out *where* the need is highest, fast enough to matter.

## The data

The corpus was roughly 45,000 disaster-related tweets spanning 14 separate events.
Raw, messy, and large enough that naive inference was a bottleneck — so I implemented
batch processing that cut inference time roughly in half, which made iterating on the
modeling actually tractable.

## Reading sentiment and place

I ran sentiment analysis using both **RoBERTa** and **VADER**, pairing a transformer's
nuance with a lexicon-based model's speed and interpretability. On top of that I built
geospatial and textual models to identify the locations of highest need, using word
frequency analysis and sentiment scoring to pull actionable signals out of the noise —
turning a stream of individual posts into a map of where attention should go.
