LLMs are famously shaky at chess — they'll confidently suggest illegal or losing moves
because they're pattern-matching language, not reasoning over the board. As lead
developer I built a RAG system that gives the model the context it's missing, so it can
coach a player through a position instead of guessing.

## The problem

Ask a raw LLM to explain a position and it has no grounding — no memory of how similar
positions have been handled, no structured sense of the tactics and strategy in play.
The coaching quality suffers accordingly.

## The RAG approach

Instead of leaning on the model's parametric knowledge, I extracted tactical and
strategic features from each board position and used them to retrieve **similar
historical positions and concept templates**. Feeding that retrieved context into the
model grounds its reasoning in real, relevant precedent rather than vibes.

## Evaluating it honestly

The interesting engineering was in measurement. I developed an offline evaluation
pipeline over **2M+ positions** to test multiple LLMs, with and without the generated
hints, so improvements were attributable rather than anecdotal.

That harness is what let me show the retrieval was actually pulling its weight — a 20%
improvement in agent performance on the evaluation set.
