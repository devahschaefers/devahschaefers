Getting an image down from a satellite is a deceptively hard systems problem. The link
is intermittent, lossy, and shared, and you don't get to ask for a retransmit on your
own schedule. I engineered the satellite's end-to-end image data stream — everything
from the bits on the wire to the image that lands intact on the ground.

## The problem

Payload links — laser and RF — drop bits and break mid-transfer. Without protection, a
single corrupted chunk can ruin an entire image, and bandwidth is far too precious to
just resend everything. The stream needed to be reliable *and* efficient at the same
time.

## The pipeline

I implemented encoding and decoding pipelines that combine compression, error
correction, and encryption. Compression makes the most of scarce bandwidth, error
correction lets the receiver repair damage without a round trip, and encryption keeps
the payload secure end to end.

<Metric value="~40%" label="effective throughput gain" />

Together that pushed effective data throughput up by about 40% and added error
detection and correction for reliable payload transmission over both link types.

## Down to the metal

The stream also had to talk to real hardware. I integrated low-level USB/UART drivers
on Linux using FTD2XX and Xilinx tooling, supporting custom baud rates and robust
streaming with retry handling for interrupted transfers — so the pipeline degraded
gracefully instead of falling over when a link hiccupped.
